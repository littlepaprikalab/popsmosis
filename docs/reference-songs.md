# Reference songs

Lyrics the owner holds up as what a Popsmosis song can be, kept with the style each was sung in. A style here is
kept by name in `studio/styles.py` (`REFERENCE_STYLES`): `python -m studio plan "<topic>" --style <name>` asks for
it as the learner's style. Cash in the Carrier's is also in the rotation a droplet gets when the learner asks for
no style (the owner, Oct 31); Split the Water's is kept by name only.

## Cash in the Carrier (`cash-in-the-carrier`)

The owner's favourite (Oct 31). AP Biology, cellular respiration, as exam review: one frame all the way through
(money: "in the bank", "that's the score", "cash in", "deal", "NAD-plus back in change"), a title line that is the
frame and the core idea at once, a pre-chorus that busts the misconception ("Little ATP so far, don't get it
twisted / The energy's in the carriers"), the story in order from the cytoplasm to the synthase, and rapped verses
that carry what the exam asks: the counts, the numbers and the yield worked out in the bridge. Every claim and
number in it checks out. Our brief, as it stood on Oct 31, would have kept out most of what makes it work (one
number at most, no run of terms, rapped lines to about 14 syllables, 3 to 5 ideas): an exam-review rap is a job of
its own. Tried in the brief on Oct 31 and undone on Nov 1: in a video, the owner found a review rap's detail more
than a student keeps from a song (docs/test-rounds.md).

Style: Hip-hop pop, upbeat 100 bpm groove, confident female rap verses with a catchy sung chorus, punchy 808s and
bright synth hook, tight quantized drum programming with crisp kick-snare hits and precise vocal layering (stored
with "clear diction" added).

## Split the Water, Catch the Light (`split-the-water`)

The owner's (Oct 31): its title line is the one the owner holds up as catchy, and the brief's first title example.
AP Biology, the light reactions. The same shape as Cash in the Carrier, rapped verses carrying the exam's detail
and a big sung chorus, with a money frame ("You can't spend sunshine", "the thylakoid trades it", "that's the
deal"). What it does well:

- **The title line is the chorus's first and last line**, so it is sung seven times, and it is the core idea in
  six words: two actions on real things, in the order they happen.
- **The chorus walks the path in order**: split the water, the electrons down the line, the protons pumped, the
  synthase spun, ATP and NADPH out.
- **The verses carry the counts and the surprises a student is asked about:** photosystem II goes first though
  it is "number two"; two H2O give four protons, four electrons and an O2; chemiosmosis is "the same trick as the
  mitochondria".
- **The pre-chorus busts the misconception with a fact**: "The oxygen you breathe / Came from water, not CO2".
- **The bridge is a sung exam question** (an FRQ): a weed killer blocks the chain right after photosystem II, and
  each line walks one effect, to "Sugar stops, the plant can't grow".

Where it leaves our brief: the intro opens on "Light comes in, water splits" rather than the title line, and the
chorus is five lines. One line a teacher would tighten: "Every hand-off pumps a proton into the lumen" (only the
cytochrome complex pumps; the other hand-offs pass the electron on). The rest checks out.

Style: bright pop with hip-hop verses, rapped verses, big sing-along chorus, clear female vocal, shimmering
synths, claps, 104 bpm, clear diction. Kept by name, not in the rotation.
