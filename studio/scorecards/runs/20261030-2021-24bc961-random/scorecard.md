### ww1-causes: why World War I started (World History, high school)

Audience high school; 96 bpm. Core idea: Alliances chained Europe's tense powers together, so one assassination pulled them into war one by one.
Title line: "One shot, the chain pulls Europe to war"

- **Hook 4.** The title line "One shot, the chain pulls Europe to war" is concrete (a shot, a chain) and carries the core idea in miniature, and it opens and closes the chorus. But "Europe to war" is a little abstract, and "the chain" is somewhat general. The lesson opens on a real question ("How did one assassination drag all of Europe into war?"), but the narration opens on a dated scene ("On June 28, 1914, Archduke Franz Ferdinand rode...") rather than something from the learner's world.
- **Frame 3.** The schoolyard where "every kid has promised to back up a friend in any fight" fits the whole job of alliances, and the lesson ties the "two kids" and "promises" to Austria-Hungary, Serbia and the alliance system. The lesson also flags where it breaks down (Britain's loose Entente). However, the song sets it up only in v1-1/v1-2 and then drops it for the chain image, and the narration does not return to it after n7. The chorus runs on "chain" instead, so two pictures compete a little.
- **Why chain 4.** Each link is taught with its cause in order: "Russia began mobilizing to protect Serbia, so Germany, seeing a threat, declared war"; "The quickest road to France ran through neutral Belgium, so German troops marched in"; "Britain had promised... so it declared war." The weak spot is the Austria-Hungary step, where the blank check and the ultimatum only loosely explain why war followed. The jump from Germany's declaration on Russia to why Germany also declared on France is explained only by the two-front plan.
- **Key lines 3.** They run in order and mostly use arrows to show cause and effect ("Russia mobilizes → Germany declares war on Russia, France"; "Belgium invaded → Britain declares war on Germany"). Problems: the first is a question, not a step; "Schlieffen Plan sends German troops through neutral Belgium" has no cause; and "Militarism, imperialism, nationalism" is a list. Starts are off in places: the Franz Ferdinand line starts on n16 ("Now back to the shooting") and "Germany's blank check → Austria-Hungary declares war" starts at the blank check, not the declaration. The Japan line sits beyond the chain and the core idea never appears as a closing line.
- **Accuracy 4.** The facts are right: the dates (June 28, July 28, August 1, 3, 4), the blank check, the 1839 Treaty of London, Britain's loose Entente and the 37 days all check out. The song's "Five great powers locked in a war" is fine. The chief problem is the song's "Each promise pulls in one power more", which blurs that Britain was bound by the Belgium treaty, not the Entente, while the "Two armed camps, the promises made" line sets Britain as a firm member of one camp. The key line that leaves out alliances is a small slip.
- **Singability 3.** Many lines are singable chants with clean rhymes: "One shot, the chain pulls Europe to war", "Tense, armed rivals, tied up tight / One by one, they're in the fight." But the verses read like a timeline, with caption lines such as "Germany: war on Russia, then France" and "Thirty-seven days since that shot was heard", and several run well past 10 syllables ("Troops through Belgium, Britain keeps its word" is fine, but "Empires draw troops from lands they command" is wordy). Some rhymes bend: "Franz Ferdinand's shot, now who's to blame?" and "the front won't budge" with "stuck". The bridge crams the Japan, Ottoman and trench-war facts.
- **Lesson 4.** It opens on a question, uses the schoolyard picture, builds on the cause links, asks a predict-then-answer question ("what do you think its plan was?"), names the misconception and says why it is wrong, and ends on a check that needs the core idea ("If there had been no alliance system, why might the fight... have stayed a local war?"). The MAIN table and the late section on Japan, the Ottomans and trenches add terms and drift toward a list of facts. The check is also answerable from the opening schoolyard picture alone. The "Got a follow-up question?" line is a stray.
- Error: Key line: "Militarism, imperialism, nationalism → rival great powers"; also the lesson's MAIN list, with alliances listed as a long-term tension: The key line leaves out alliances, which are the first letter of MAIN and the idea's core, and the chain's step 1 lists only three tensions, so the line and the lesson table differ. Correction: Use "Militarism, alliances, imperialism, nationalism → rival great powers", or note that the alliances link follows next.
- Error: Lyric p1-1/p1-2 and narration n20-n21: "the long-term tensions turned a local fight into a European war" sits right after the declaration on Serbia: The misconception is placed before the spread, which is fine, but narration n21 says the tensions turned it into a "European war" before the story of how it spread has been told. Correction: Move the misconception after Britain joins, or say the tensions made a local fight likely to spread.

  - v1-8 lyric-style: "Franz Ferdinand's shot, now who's to blame?" runs 11 syllables, over 10
  - p2-2 lyric-style: "But the chain keeps pulling past Europe's shore" runs 11 syllables, over 10

### mitosis: how mitosis makes two identical cells (Biology, high school)

Audience high school; 92 bpm. Core idea: A cell copies each chromosome, then spindle fibers pull one copy of each to opposite sides before splitting.
Title line: "Copy the page, deal one each way"

- **Hook 4.** The title line "Copy the page, deal one each way" is concrete, singable and captures the whole move (copy, then deal out), and it opens and closes the chorus. The lesson opens on a real question, "How does mitosis make two identical cells?", and the narration opens on "Scrape your knee", which is from the learner's world. It loses a little because "Copy the page" is not obviously about cells without the frame, and the lesson's second sentence turns to a definition.
- **Frame 4.** The recipe-book picture (photocopy each page, clip it to its copy, deal one of each pair into two binders) does the job of the whole process, and it carries through the intro, chorus, outro, lesson and narration (n22 "That's the dealing"). The nondisjunction bridge and metaphase checkpoint sit inside it only loosely, and the bridge lets the picture go. The mapping of pages and clips to chromosomes and chromatids is slightly off: a page is a chromosome but the 'copy' is a chromatid, and the song never says which is which.
- **Why chain 4.** All five links are taught in order with their causes: "so each pole can receive one chromatid of every chromosome", "so the two copies will be pulled in different directions", and "so mitosis only has to separate the copies". The narration adds "That matters, because now each copy will be pulled a different way" and "Lined up like this, every chromosome is ready to split evenly". The song carries the gist. The last link, a nucleus forming and the cell dividing so that both daughters hold the full set, is stated more as sequence than as cause.
- **Key lines 3.** Most lines are steps in order, such as "Fibers from opposite poles attach, one per sister chromatid" and "Anaphase: chromatids separate → one copy of each per pole". But the first is only the title question, and the second, "Page = chromosome, clip = centromere, binder = daughter cell", is a label. "Metaphase: chromosomes lined up single file across the middle" is also a label with no 'so', and it starts at n16 "Back on the path, metaphase comes next", which is a transition and not the step. The key line "Cell divides → two daughter cells, 46 chromosomes each" starts at v2-7 and n25, where the cell is only pinching in two. Telophase and the core idea are missing from the chain.
- **Accuracy 4.** The facts are mostly correct for high school: copying in interphase, 46 chromosomes in each daughter cell, anaphase separation, nondisjunction and the metaphase checkpoint. There are two small imprecisions. "all it does is pull the pairs apart" overstates what mitosis does. Attachment is placed in prophase, where it usually belongs to prometaphase, and the checkpoint is called a metaphase checkpoint, which matches the course. Nothing a learner would carry away wrong.
- **Singability 3.** Many lines are short and sung-like: "Copy the page, deal one each way", "Two cells the same, that's how they're made". The chorus is a strong chant. But some lines read as captions or textbook sentences: "Prophase hooks, metaphase lines them straight" (misrhymes with "away"/"same" loosely), "Forty-six in a skin cell, here's the twist", "Each new cell still ends with forty-six" (a slant rhyme), and the bridge's "The two don't match and may not work right". Several lines run past 10 syllables, such as "Centromere splits, the twins divide" and the bridge lines. The terms prophase, metaphase, anaphase and cytokinesis are stacked in the chorus, which makes it a list.
- **Lesson 4.** It opens on a question, gives the recipe-book picture, builds step by step with causes, asks "What do you think happens when the fibers start to pull?", names the misconception and ends on "Why does each daughter cell end up with the full 46 chromosomes". But the prediction question comes just before step 4 without being answered right away in a clearly marked way, and the nondisjunction and meiosis sections bring in new terms (nondisjunction, checkpoint, cleavage furrow, cell plate) that a first-time learner may not need. The misconception comes late. The closing check is answerable from the misconception paragraph as well as from understanding.
- Error: Lyric p1-1/p1-2: "Interphase copies, then mitosis starts / And all it does is pull the pairs apart"; also narration n8 and key line "Interphase copies DNA": Mitosis does more than pull pairs apart: it also condenses chromosomes, forms the spindle and rebuilds nuclei and divides the cell. This is a loose simplification, though the lesson says "mitosis only separates the copies", so it is the course's own wording. Correction: Say mitosis only separates the copies, without any new DNA copying, rather than 'all it does is pull the pairs apart'.
- Error: Lesson step 2 and lyric v1-5 to v1-8: spindle fibers attach in prophase: Spindle fibers grow in prophase, but attaching to the centromere (kinetochores) is usually placed in late prophase/prometaphase, so teaching it as part of prophase may mislead slightly. Correction: Say the fibers attach at the end of prophase, and say that this happens before metaphase.

  - n6 not-in-scene: names Centromere, which scene "analogy" does not draw
  - n6 not-in-scene: names Chromosomes, which scene "analogy" does not draw
  - n27 not-in-scene: names Anaphase, which scene "nondisjunction" does not draw
  - n31 not-in-scene: names Anaphase, which scene "nondisjunction" does not draw
  - n31 not-in-scene: names Spindle fibers, which scene "nondisjunction" does not draw

  - b-4 lyric-style: "The other's one short when the deal is done" runs 11 syllables, over 10

### entropy: what entropy is (Thermodynamics, college)

Audience high school; 110 bpm. Core idea: Entropy counts the ways particles and energy can be arranged; things spread out because spread-out arrangements vastly outnumber concentrated ones.
Title line: "Roll the dice and count the ways"

- **Hook 4.** The title line "Roll the dice and count the ways" is concrete, singable after one hearing, and names the counting idea, though it names the analogy and the counting rather than the big move of spreading out. The lesson opens on a real question the learner asked, "If entropy isn't really disorder, what is it?", which is strong. The narration opens with the hot coffee in n1-n2, which is from the learner's world, though n3 then brings in "disorder".
- **Frame 3.** The dice picture (faces = microstate, total = macrostate) is apt for counting ways and is set up in the verse and carried through the chorus "Roll the dice and count the ways", the lesson and the narration. But it only explains the counting link, not what the whole thing is for, which is why things spread out and never return. After v1 the song moves to the box, coffee and freezer, and the dice drop out of the narration after n10. The bridge freezer and the coffee have no connection to the dice frame.
- **Why chain 4.** The lesson and narration walk through the links in order: many microstates per macrostate, entropy as the count, random hopping ("constantly hopping from one microstate to another"), spread-out states outnumbering concentrated ones (4 molecules, 16 arrangements; 100 molecules, 1 in 10^30), and then the second law. Each link says why, and "Nothing pushes it" is explained by counting. The coffee and freezer extensions add the Q/T rule, which is a different route to the same conclusion and adds a little load. The song carries the gist.
- **Key lines 2.** The lines mostly follow the chain: dice faces = microstate, entropy = number of microstates, random motion, outnumbering, second law. But the first, "If entropy isn't really disorder, what is it?", is a question rather than a step, and it starts at i-1, "Roll the dice and count the ways". "Entropy counts microstates, not messiness" is a slogan with no narration anchor. "Spread-out arrangements vastly outnumber crowded ones → gas spreads" starts at v2-1, "A hundred molecules, all left?", which is a different step. "Freezer's motor stops → heat flows in → ice melts" starts at b-1, where the line sung is "A freezer turns water into ice". The core idea is never stated as a line, and the end of the chain is not stated either.
- **Accuracy 4.** The physics is sound at the level: S = k ln W, 16 arrangements, 1 in 10^30 for 100 molecules (2^-100 ≈ 8×10^-31), and the Q/T coffee analysis. The remarks that messy and tidy rooms have almost the same entropy are fair. The one imprecision is v2-4, "Entropy climbs and never drops", which drops the isolated-system condition. The song's b-6 and b-7 "heat runs hot to cold" are fine.
- **Singability 3.** The chorus is a clean chant that can be sung back, with a repeated title line and the rhyme ways/stay. The dice verse ("Two dice land, a three, a four / Seven's the total on the floor") is singable. But several lines read as captions or textbook sentences: "Same rule caps a heat engine's best / How efficient it can be: what's next" is forced and sits outside the idea; "Seal the box and lift the partition" and "Entropy counts the ways, like you do" has a filler tag; "Coffee's entropy dips, air's climbs more" is dense. The bridge runs eight lines of explanation, and the third chorus is longer with the lines reordered.
- **Lesson 3.** It opens with a question, builds on counting with dice, and has a predict-the-answer moment ("What do you think pushes the gas into the empty half? Nothing pushes it") and names the disorder misconception clearly. It ends on a check that requires the core idea: "why does the gas spread... even though nothing pushes it?". But it carries many terms for a high school first-timer (microstate, macrostate, isolated system, equilibrium, Boltzmann's equation, Clausius's rule, ΔS = Q/T), and the freezer and heat-engine paragraph at the end could be cut. The misconception comes after the main explanation rather than at the start.
- Error: Lesson: "As energy flows from the hot coffee into the cooler room air, it spreads among more particles, which raises the total number of microstates." alongside "The hot coffee loses heat Q at a high temperature, so its entropy drops a little.": Presenting 'more particles' as the main reason and then a separate Q/T rule is fine, but the lesson also says heat "never flows on its own from a colder body to a hotter one" as a cause of cooling, which circles back to the second law rather than explaining it. Correction: Say the coffee cools because the energy spreading into the room air raises total entropy more than the coffee loses, and heat flowing from cold to hot would lower total entropy.
- Error: Song c3-1/c3-2: "Dice that land, a cup that cools / Total entropy climbs, that's the rule"; v2-4 "Entropy climbs and never drops": "Entropy climbs and never drops" is stated without the isolated-system condition, so a learner could take it as true for any part (the song itself later shows the water's entropy dropping). Correction: Say "an isolated system's entropy never drops" or "total entropy never drops".
- Checks left for the prompt:
  - v2-7 lyric-style: "Coffee's entropy dips, air's climbs more" runs 11 syllables, over 10
  - b-2 lyric-style: "Water's entropy drops, but there's a price" runs 12 syllables, over 10
  - b-4 lyric-style: "So the kitchen's entropy climbs more there" runs 11 syllables, over 10
  - c3-2 lyric-style: "Total entropy climbs, that's the rule" runs 11 syllables, over 10
