### ww1-causes: why World War I started (World History, high school)

Audience high school; 96 bpm. Core idea: A single assassination became a world war because alliances and war plans pulled each great power in, one after another.
Title line: "One shot fired, the allies pile in"

- **Hook 4.** The title line "One shot fired, the allies pile in" is concrete and singable, and it is the core idea in miniature, with real actions (shot, pile in). "The allies" is slightly generic, and the lesson opens on a real question, "How did one shooting in Sarajevo start a world war?". The narration opens on stone memorials in towns, which is from the learner's world but is a distant, somber entry rather than a pull.
- **Frame 4.** The street scuffle, "both fighters have friends who promised to back them up. One shove, and the friends pile in", does the same job as the whole causal process and maps the parts (shove, fighters, friends) inside it. It is set up in the song's verse 1 and returns in the title line and the outro ("A crowded street, one shove, one fight"), and the lesson and narration use it. It drops out of the second verse, pre-choruses and bridge, and the lesson does not say where the picture breaks (for example, nations had treaty obligations and war plans, not just loyalty).
- **Why chain 4.** The lesson and narration teach each link with its cause: "Russia saw itself as Serbia's protector, so it began mobilization", "Germany... treated that as a threat", and the Schlieffen Plan through Belgium leading to Britain's 1839 promise. The step from Russian mobilization to Germany's war declaration is thin: why mobilization threatened Germany, given its two-front plan, is only implied. The second link also skips why Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia, though nationalism and Black Hand ties are partly given. The song carries the gist well.
- **Key lines 3.** The lines read as a chain from the question through alliances, assassination, Austria's war, Russia, Germany, and Belgium/Britain. Several are labels or notation rather than steps: "Shove = assassination; fighters = Austria-Hungary and Serbia" and "Assassination = spark...". The first line is the question, not a step, and the last line (stalls at the Marne) goes beyond the core idea. Some start points do not match the step: "Nationalism → Princip kills Franz Ferdinand" is attached to a narration line about the open car, and the Schlieffen line starts on the Belgium lyric against a narration line about the plan's aim. The "Britain declares war" step has no line of its own.
- **Accuracy 4.** The facts are largely right: the dates, the 1839 treaty, Princip's age, the alliances, and the Marne. The one real imprecision is the Germany-France link, which the song and lesson present as an ally-chain when the Schlieffen Plan was the cause. The "Triple Alliance" is shown with Italy, and the lesson omits that Italy stayed neutral in the main text, though facts mention it. Nothing a learner would carry away badly wrong.
- **Singability 3.** Many lines are short and chantable with good call-and-response in the chorus: "One shot fired, the allies pile in / Pledges, war plans, the world war begins". Some verse lines read like textbook captions: "Germany says that's a threat, so war", "Russia's move? Mobilization!", "Huge army called across the nation". There are several forced rhymes ("Serbia yearns", "Serbia's named", "Britain" with "pile in", "war" with "ally more"). The bridge is nearly all narration, with "That same day, war from Britain" grammatically bent to fit.
- **Lesson 3.** The lesson opens on a question, gives the big picture with the street image, and builds step by step on causes. It has a prediction prompt ("What do you think Germany did when Russia mobilized?") answered immediately, and it names the misconception. However, the misconception is placed mid-list, splitting steps 5 and 6 awkwardly; the numbered list gets close to a list of parts; the many terms (alliance system, militarism, nationalism, mobilization, Schlieffen, dreadnoughts) are heavy; and the final check ("Why did a quarrel between Austria-Hungary and Serbia end with Britain at war with Germany?") is good but can be answered by repeating the Belgium step.
- Error: Lesson step 5 / narration n23-n24: "Germany, Austria-Hungary's ally, treated that as a threat: it declared war on Russia on 1 August" and chain link 4 that Germany then declared war on France as 'Russia's ally': This slightly overstates the reason: Germany declared war on France mainly because the Schlieffen Plan required beating France first, not just because France was Russia's ally. Correction: Say Germany declared war on France because its war plan required defeating France before turning on Russia.
- Error: Song c1-4 / c3-6: "Five great powers, five weeks in" and lyric v2-3 "One month later, war's declared": The assassination was 28 June and Austria's declaration was 28 July, so "one month later" is fine; but the "five weeks" counts the assassination to 4 August, which makes the 'five great powers' fall within the five weeks only because Britain joined on day 37, a fair round number. No real issue beyond rounding. Correction: No change needed beyond optionally saying 'about five weeks'.

### mitosis: how mitosis makes two identical cells (Biology, high school)

Audience high school; 92 bpm. Core idea: The cell copies every chromosome, then pulls the copies apart so each new cell gets a full set.
Title line: "Copy the chromosomes, split in two"

- **Hook 5.** The title line "Copy the chromosomes, split in two" is concrete, singable after one hearing, and names the big move (copy, then split). It is close to the core idea in miniature, though "split in two" is a little vague about what splits. The lesson opens on a good question, "How does mitosis make two identical cells?", and the narration opens on a scraped knee, which comes from the learner's world.
- **Frame 4.** The library opening a second branch is an apt picture of the whole job: copy every book, clip each copy to its original, send one of each pair to each branch. It is set up early in the lesson and narration, and the song brings it in at v1-1 and the outro ("Copy every book, a branch brand new"). But the song drops the picture in the chorus, bridge and second verse, which are mostly plain terminology. Lines like "Cytokinesis: two new homes" only hint at it.
- **Why chain 4.** The lesson and narration teach every link in order, each with its cause: copy in interphase, so two sisters; fibers attach one to each, "so each sister is tied to a different pole"; then anaphase, "so each pole ends up with one copy of every chromosome". Metaphase is weakly explained, because the droplet never says why lining up gives an even split. The prediction question sits oddly after metaphase, and the telophase step is treated mostly as description. The song carries the gist, and the bridge adds the nondisjunction contrast.
- **Key lines 3.** Read in order they follow the chain: interphase copy, the misconception line, spindle fibers tying each sister to a different pole, anaphase "sisters split → each pole gets every chromosome", then cytokinesis. The first line is a question rather than a step, and the second ("Books = chromosomes...") is a label. Metaphase is also a label with no because. Nondisjunction goes beyond the core chain. Several start in sensible places, but the interphase line is split from the misconception line, and "Cytokinesis splits the cell → two identical daughter cells" starts at v2-8 properly.
- **Accuracy 4.** The science is largely correct: DNA copied in interphase, spindle fibers attach one to each sister, sisters split in anaphase, nondisjunction, and 46 chromosomes. The song's v1-7 and v1-8 jumble the order by putting prophase coiling inside the interphase verse. Also "Mitosis splits them, never makes" is a slight overstatement but acceptable. The narration's n22 and n21 are out of order in the listing, though that is only an ordering issue. One imprecision a teacher would tighten.
- **Singability 3.** Many lines are short and chantable ("Copy the chromosomes, split in two", "Spindle fibers reach from either side"), and the chorus is easy to sing back. The rhymes are mostly clean, though "two" / "new" is repeated. Some lines read like textbook captions: "Interphase comes before the split", "Metaphase lines them up single file", "Meiosis is next, it halves the count". Others strain the rhyme or the meaning: "p1-2 Mitosis splits them, never makes" is an unfinished thought, and "To make the sex cells, a different route" does not rhyme with "count". The bridge runs past what is natural for a bridge in places, such as b-3 and b-8.
- **Lesson 4.** It opens on a question and the knee example, gives the big idea and the library picture, builds step by step on why, names the misconception and explains why it is wrong, and asks a prediction question that is answered right after ("what do you think happens when the sisters let go? They split apart..."). The closing check, "If a cell skipped interphase... why couldn't both of its daughter cells get a full set?", can only be answered by someone who understands the core idea. The weaknesses: the prediction question comes after metaphase, which is slightly awkward, and the term load is high for a first read (centromere, cleavage furrow, cell plate, nondisjunction, telophase).
- Error: Lyric v1-7 "Then they coil up tight enough to see" sits right after the interphase verse with no prophase named; also v1-8 "Forty-six of them in you and me": The coiling is in prophase and is placed under interphase, so it implies the chromosomes become visible during copying. And the chromosomes number 46 before copying, but after copying there are 46 chromosomes of two chromatids each, which the line does not distinguish. Correction: Put the coiling after interphase and label it as prophase, or cut it from the interphase verse.
- Error: Lyric v2-4 "Right across the middle, like an aisle" and v2-3 "Metaphase lines them up single file": This is minor; chromosomes line up at the metaphase plate, but 'single file' is a fair simplification, so it is not really an error. Correction: No change needed.

### entropy: what entropy is (Thermodynamics, college)

Audience high school; 110 bpm. Core idea: Entropy counts the ways energy can be arranged; energy spreads out because spread-out states have vastly more ways.
Title line: "Count the ways the heat can spread"

- **Hook 4.** The title line "Count the ways the heat can spread" is concrete, singable after one hearing and opens and closes the chorus, so it is close to the core idea in miniature. It names counting and spreading, though "count the ways" is abstract and the line has no real thing in it to act on. The lesson opens on the learner's own question, "If entropy isn't disorder, what is it?", which is what they asked. The narration opens on hot coffee cooling, from the learner's world, but the question about disorder is not picked up until n24.
- **Frame 3.** The two dice are the frame: "Each die's a particle... Seven beats two at six times the rate." They carry the counting idea well and recur in the song's intro, chorus c3-1 and outro. But they picture only the counting part, not what the whole thing is for, which is energy spreading. After v1 the dice nearly vanish from the lesson and narration, which move to packets and coffee. The coffee-cooling example fits the dice only loosely, and the fridge and ice material sit outside any picture.
- **Why chain 4.** The lesson teaches every link in order, each with its cause: random passing makes arrangements equally likely, more ways turn up more often, spreading has more ways (5 vs 35), huge numbers make it a law, and so heat flows one way. The narration follows the same order, and n15–n16 ask and answer the prediction. Link 3 is a little loose: the 5-vs-35 example is the number of ways, and the narration then says "like rolling a seven" without showing that the spread macrostate beats the concentrated one. The link between the second law and heat flowing one way is made by the numbers section more than by the chain. The song carries the gist.
- **Key lines 2.** Several lines are real steps: "Random collisions → every arrangement equally likely", "More microstates → that macrostate turns up more often" and "Spread over more molecules → vastly more ways". But the first, "If entropy isn't disorder, what is it?", is a question, not a step. "Entropy counts ways, not messiness" has no narration anchor and is a slogan. "Second law limits heat engine efficiency: next topic" is off-chain, and "Fridge loses power..." is a side example. The "Dice: exact faces = microstate" line is a label. The core idea never lands as a closing line, and the chain's final link, hot to cold and never back, is missing.
- **Accuracy 4.** The physics is sound: S = k ln W, the 5 and 35 counts are right (C(5,1)=5 and C(7,4)=35 for packets on 2 and 4 molecules), the 1800 J example gives −5 and +6 J/K, and the card-deck and ice points are correct for thermodynamic entropy. There is one real weakness: the 5-vs-35 comparison is built on a different number of molecules, and the narration changes between four molecules and two. The song's "neat ice forms, total entropy's raised" drops the cause. The song's bridge line "the hot-to-cold way" for heat flowing from the warmer kitchen to the shelf is fine. Overall, one imprecision a teacher would tighten.
- **Singability 3.** The chorus is a strong chant ("Count the ways the heat can spread / More ways spread, so that's where it heads") with clean rhymes and a repeated title. But much of the song is textbook sentences set in rhyme: "Each die's a particle, toss them high / The total's the macrostate you spy", "Four packets on two molecules: five", "Coffee drops five, the room air gains six / One more joule per kelvin in the mix" and "Heat engines are next in line / The second law caps how well they shine". Several lines run past 10 syllables, and "the odds stack up" and "in the mix" are filler tags. The lines are mostly rhythmic and rhymed, so this is better than prose, but verse 2 and the pre-chorus read like captions.
- **Lesson 3.** It opens on the learner's question and gives the big picture right away, and the dice give a first-time learner a way in. It asks one prediction ("What do you think happens when coffee and air molecules pass packets at random...?") and answers it right after, and it names the misconception and gives a reason. The closing check, "Why does heat almost always flow from hot coffee into cool air, when energy would be conserved either way?", can only be answered by someone who understood. But it carries many terms for high school (microstate, macrostate, energy packets, second law, S = k ln W, Boltzmann's constant, Q/T, first law), and the numbers section and the heat-engine line could be cut. The misconception paragraph is placed in the middle of the chain, between steps 4 and 5, which breaks its flow.
- Error: Lesson step 3 / narration n18: "4 energy packets kept on 2 molecules can be arranged in only 5 ways, but spread over 4 molecules in 35 ways" (narration n17 says "four molecules sharing four energy packets" and then n18 "Kept on the two coffee molecules"): The 5-vs-35 comparison is not like-for-like with the physical situation. Comparing different numbers of molecules does not show that spreading beats staying concentrated within a fixed system, and n17 says four molecules while n18 puts the same packets on two. The 35 is also not a spread-out macrostate competing with the 5 as an equal-probability alternative. In the narration the "two coffee molecules" are one part of the four, so the 5 ways are a subset of the 35, which is mostly fine, but the wording suggests 5 ways are available when the packets stay in the coffee. Correction: Say that within the four molecules there are 35 arrangements in total, and only 5 of them keep all four packets on the two coffee molecules, so about 86% of arrangements have the energy spread to the air.
- Error: Lesson: "Water even freezes into orderly ice below 0 °C, because the heat it releases raises the colder surroundings' entropy by more..."; pre-chorus p1-2: "Neat ice forms, total entropy's raised": The song states the conclusion with no cause, and the lesson treats freezing as raising the surroundings' entropy because heat is released, without saying that the surroundings must be colder than the water. The lesson does say "colder", so only the song misleads. Correction: Keep the lesson's reasoning; in the song say something like "Ice forms, the heat it sheds spreads out".
- Error: Narration n9: "Entropy counts those ways, so seven, with more microstates, comes up six times as often.": Entropy counts microstates, but the claim that seven comes up six times as often than two depends on the dice being fair, which n14 only says afterward. It is a minor ordering flaw. Correction: State that each pair of faces is equally likely before saying seven comes up six times as often.
- Checks left for the prompt:
  - p1-2 lyric-style: "Neat ice forms, total entropy's raised" runs 11 syllables, over 10
