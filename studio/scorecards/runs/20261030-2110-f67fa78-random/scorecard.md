### ww1-causes: why World War I started (World History, high school)

Audience high school; 96 bpm. Core idea: A local assassination became a world war because alliances pulled great powers in, one after another.
Title line: "Two shots fired, great powers pulled in"

- **Hook 4.** The title line "Two shots fired, great powers pulled in" is concrete and memorable, with real actions (shots fired, powers pulled in), and it is the core idea in miniature. It opens and closes the chorus. It is a little passive and it names the cause and effect without the rope, so it is not quite the big move. The lesson opens on "How did one assassination start a war across Europe?" and the narration opens on town war memorials, which is from the learner's world.
- **Frame 4.** The roped climbers ("The rope is meant to stop a fall, but if one climber slips, the rope can drag the others down") do the same job as alliances and fit the whole idea, and the Italy section ("the rope never pulled") fits inside it. The lesson and narration carry it with headers like "The slip" and "The rope pulls". The song sets it up in v1-1 and uses it in the chorus ("One rope ties them, the drag begins"), the bridge and the outro. It thins out in verse 2 and the pre-choruses, where the rope is not mentioned.
- **Why chain 4.** The lesson and narration give each link with its cause: "Russia saw itself as Serbia's protector, so it began mobilization", "The quickest road to Paris ran through neutral Belgium, so the German army marched into Belgium", and "Britain had promised to protect Belgium's neutrality, so... declared war". The Italy example shows the alliance mechanism. One gap is that the step from Russia's mobilization to Germany declaring war on Russia and France is thin ("saw a threat"), and why France is included is not explained until the Schlieffen Plan. The song carries the chain's gist.
- **Key lines 3.** In order they tell the story from the opening question through alliances, the spark, Germany's backing, mobilization, the Schlieffen Plan, Belgium and Britain, and Italy. Most are steps with arrows, and they are short. Some weaknesses: the first is a question, not a step, and the last, "Austria-Hungary attacked first, so Italy stayed neutral", ends on a side case and not on the core idea. The Schlieffen line starts at n22, which is the Schlieffen Plan, but the Germany-declares-war link sits on the Russia line, whose lyric v2-3 does not cover Germany's declaration. "Gavrilo Princip kills..." starts on the "open car" line, before the shooting is named.
- **Accuracy 4.** The facts, dates and sequence (June 28, July 28, August 1, 3 and 4; Bosnia annexed 1908; Italy neutral in 1914 and joining in 1915) are right. The main imprecisions are the thin explanation for why Germany declared war on France and the simplified framing of the alliances as a single rope. The song also says "Austria strikes and Russia calls", which is fine. Nothing a learner would carry away wrong, but a teacher would tighten the Germany-France link.
- **Singability 3.** The chorus is a strong, repeatable chant: "Two shots fired, great powers pulled in / One rope ties them, the drag begins". Many verse lines read like captions or lists, such as "Armies growing: that's militarism", "Serbs against Austria: nationalism" and "Schlieffen Plan: through Belgium, fast advance". Rhymes are sometimes bent ("Austria attacks" with "we've got your back"; "advance"/"France"; "no call"), and several lines run long, such as "Help a partner if attacked, that's all". Verse 1 also drifts into labels.
- **Lesson 4.** It opens on the question "How did one assassination start a war across Europe?", gives the big picture with the roped-team image, and builds in cause-and-effect order. It asks "What do you think Germany... did next?" and answers it straight away. It names the misconception and why it is wrong, and ends with a check that needs the core idea. It carries many terms and names for a first-time learner (eight key terms, plus the table), and the Italy section plus the table add length without much gain.
- Error: "Austria-Hungary had attacked first, so Italy stayed neutral in 1914" (lesson, narration n27-n28, key line, bridge b-3 to b-4): It is the standard account, but Italy's neutrality is presented as flowing straight from the treaty's defensive terms, and it also did not want to fight, which is left out. Correction: Italy argued the alliance was defensive and Austria-Hungary had started the war, so it declared neutrality; it also had its own territorial interests against Austria-Hungary.
- Error: "Germany saw a threat, so it declared war on Russia on August 1, then on France on August 3" (narration n20; lesson step 4): This implies the Triple Alliance's rope pulled Germany in, but Germany's alliance with Austria-Hungary was not the reason it declared war on France; France was attacked because of the Schlieffen Plan. Correction: Germany declared war on Russia over its mobilization, then on France because the Schlieffen Plan required defeating France first.

### mitosis: how mitosis makes two identical cells (Biology, high school)

Audience high school; 92 bpm. Core idea: The cell copies every chromosome first, then mitosis pulls the copies apart evenly, so both new cells match.
Title line: "Copy each chromosome, split the pair"

- **Hook 4.** The title line "Copy each chromosome, split the pair" is concrete, built from actions on real things, and sings back easily. It names the copy-then-split move, though it leaves out the even pull-apart. The lesson opens on "How does one cell split into two identical cells?", which is a good question, and the narration opens on "When you scrape your knee", which comes from the learner's world. A small slip: the lesson's own question line is not the title, and the first key line is the lesson's question rather than a step.
- **Frame 4.** The recipe book shared between two kitchens suits the whole job of mitosis: pages photocopied, clipped to copies, then unclipped so each kitchen gets a complete book. It appears in the lesson and narration (n4-n6) and in the song's v1-1 and v1-2, and the c3-2 line "Two matching books on either side" carries it into the final chorus. It is mostly dropped in the later verses, the chorus, and the bridge, which is mostly literal chromosome talk. The lesson ties "the clips come undone" to anaphase, which is a good use of the picture.
- **Why chain 4.** The lesson and narration teach each link with its cause: "the nuclear envelope breaks down... With the envelope gone, spindle fibers... can reach the chromosomes", and "That lineup matters, because it puts one sister chromatid of every chromosome facing each pole". Anaphase's pull gives "each pole ends up with one full set". The song carries the gist in order. A few links are slightly thin: telophase and cytokinesis are described as events rather than why they make two matching cells, and the lesson's anaphase link says "so each pole ends up with one full set" after the pull, which works. Overall it is complete and ordered.
- **Key lines 3.** Several lines are real steps, such as "Prophase: envelope breaks down → spindle fibers reach chromosomes" and "Anaphase: spindle fibers shorten → sister chromatids to opposite poles". But the first line is the lesson's question, not a step, and "Pages = chromosomes, kitchens = daughter cells" is a label. Timing is off in places: the prophase line starts at v1-5 and the metaphase line at v1-6, which fits, but the first line starts at i-1, a lyric that does not ask the question, and the narration n11 "Next comes prophase" and n14 "Now zoom in on metaphase" are labels with the step coming later. The chain also lacks the core-idea line, and the nondisjunction line is an extra branch. The "Interphase" line has a good cause and effect.
- **Accuracy 4.** The science is correct for high school: copying in interphase, chromatid separation in anaphase, 46 chromosomes per pole, and nondisjunction giving 47 and 45. The one claim to tighten is the song's c1-4 "One in, two out, matching inside", which is ambiguous about what goes in and out. Nothing a learner would carry away wrong. The bridge's "One extra page, one page is gone" is an apt description of 47 and 45. The line "Meiosis next: sex cells get half" is fine.
- **Singability 3.** Many lines are short and chantable, such as the chorus "Copy each chromosome, split the pair" and "Line them up for an even share", with clean rhymes (pair/share, side/inside). But several lines read like captions: "Prophase coils them, the wall breaks down", "Interphase copies, then it begins", and the bridge's "So both new cells have the count wrong" and "Or die, one stuck pair was all it took" run long and bend grammar to rhyme. "Forty-six per pole, a full set home" is a forced rhyme tag. The stage-by-stage verses feel like a list of terms.
- **Lesson 4.** The lesson opens on a question, gives the big idea and the recipe picture, and builds step by step with causes. It names the misconception and why it is wrong. It includes a prediction prompt ("What do you think happens when the spindle fibers shorten?") answered right after. It ends on a check that needs the core idea: "Mitosis itself copies nothing, so why do both daughter cells still end up with a full, identical set?" Weaknesses: eight key terms plus centromere, cleavage furrow, cell plate and nondisjunction is a lot for a first read, and the nondisjunction section and the meiosis note are extra beyond the core idea.
- Error: Lyric v1-1 to v1-2 and the lesson: "Copy each page before they cook" / "Every page is photocopied" (recipe frame, as used in the chorus "One in, two out, matching inside", c1-4): "One in, two out" is vague and could be read as one cell yielding two, but it also suggests one chromosome in and two out, which does not match the chorus's own stated meaning. Correction: Say something like "One cell in, two cells out, matching inside".
- Error: Lyric pc1-1 to pc1-2 and v1-4: "Interphase copies, then it begins": The grammar is loose, since "it" has no clear referent, but the claim itself is accurate; no factual correction needed beyond clarity. Correction: Say "Interphase copies, then mitosis begins".

### entropy: what entropy is (Thermodynamics, college)

Audience high school; 110 bpm. Core idea: Entropy counts the ways particles and energy can be arranged; nature drifts toward states with the most ways.
Title line: "Roll the dice and count the ways"

- **Hook 4.** The title line "Roll the dice and count the ways" is concrete, singable after one hearing, and opens and closes the chorus. It names the counting move but not the nature-drifts-to-most half, so it is only part of the core idea. The lesson opens on the learner's own question, "What is entropy, really, if it isn't just disorder?", and the narration opens on a drop of food coloring, a good everyday image.
- **Frame 4.** Dice fit the whole idea: totals as macrostates, face pairs as microstates, "seven comes up six times as often" with no preference involved. They are set up in the first line of the song, return in the chorus ("Seven on the dice, gas spread wide") and the outro, and anchor the lesson and the narration. The bridge's fridge and the song's coffee-free gas verse sit outside the dice, but the dice still explain the likelihood logic, and they only loosely cover the Q/T section.
- **Why chain 5.** The lesson and the narration teach all four links in order, with causes: many microstates per macrostate, entropy as the count, random motion landing on the likeliest macrostate, and spreading as the second law. The explanation of why ("not because the dice prefer seven, but because more microstates make it") is clear. The song carries the gist well. The Q/T coffee case is an extra not in the chain, but it does not break the chain.
- **Key lines 3.** The lines mostly tell the chain, but the first, "What is entropy, really, if it isn't just disorder?", is a question and not a step. The fourth, "4 molecules: all left = 1 microstate; 2 per half = 6", is more data than step. The last, "Fridge: cold → hot (heat) needs work; total entropy rises", is not in the chain and has no narration sentence. There is also no final line stating the core idea. The middle lines ("More microstates per macrostate → higher entropy", "Random motion → the likeliest macrostate") are good steps.
- **Accuracy 4.** The core physics is right: the microstate counts (1 and 6 of 16), Boltzmann's S = k ln W, and the statistical nature of the second law. The less-than-one-in-a-million-trillion-trillion figure (2^-100 ≈ 8×10^-31) is right. The main weakness is the coffee argument, which leaves out that the same Q moves between coffee and room and is loose on the constant-temperature caveat. A teacher would tighten it, but nothing a learner carries away is wrong.
- **Singability 3.** The chorus is a strong chant: "Roll the dice and count the ways" bookends it, and gang vocals can shout it back. The verses have good call-and-answer images ("Snake eyes, just one way to roll a two / Seven's got six ways to come through"). Several lines run long or read like textbook sentences: "Less than one in a million trillion trillion" (~14 syllables), "Entropy counts the microstates", "Place the particles and energy". Rhymes like "energy / entropy" and "brilliant / trillion" are slightly forced, and the bridge reads like explanation.
- **Lesson 4.** It opens on a learner question, gives the dice picture, and builds on why. It asks a prediction question ("What do you think happens with 100 molecules...") and answers it straight after, and it names the disorder misconception. The ending check, "The second law is statistical, so why do you never see all the gas in a box crowd into one half?", needs the core idea. Weaknesses: many terms (macrostate, microstate, isolated system, Boltzmann's equation, joules per kelvin, Q/T, Gibbs) for a first-time high school reader, and the coffee section and Gibbs mention could be cut.
- Error: Lesson and narration (n25-n27): "Dividing by the smaller temperature gives a bigger result, so the room gains more entropy than the coffee loses." Also the lesson's one-case paragraph.: The coffee case works only if the same amount of heat Q leaves the coffee and enters the room; this is never stated, so the Q/T argument is incomplete. The reversible-constant-T caveat is also loosely applied, since the coffee's temperature changes as it cools. Correction: Say the same heat Q leaves the coffee and enters the room, so Q/T_room is larger than Q/T_coffee, and treat it as an approximation because the coffee's temperature changes.
