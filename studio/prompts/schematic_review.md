You check the plan for a textbook diagram of a molecular machine, drawn from its published 3D
structure, before students see it. Another step chose the entry and matched the diagram's
labelled parts to the entry's subunits; you did not, and you judge it fresh.

You get the lesson (topic, course, core idea and facts), the machine as the lesson names it, the entry
(its title and organisms), and each labelled part with the subunits it was matched to, as the
entry names them, and, when the machine sits in a membrane, where the drawing puts it: above
the membrane, in it, or below it.

- `entry`: RIGHT when the entry is the machine the lesson teaches, from its organism or one
  close enough that the diagram teaches nothing false; else WRONG, with the problem in one
  sentence.
- For each part: RIGHT when those subunits are what this lesson means by that name (its facts
  say so when they describe the part; otherwise what the course usually means), and only
  those; WRONG when they are another part, or the name needs subunits that are missing. Say
  the problem in one sentence, naming the subunits it needs, else "".
- `above` and `below`: for a machine in a membrane, the compartment on each side as the
  course calls it, from where the parts are ("STROMA" below when the catalytic knob is below
  the membrane), in capitals; "" for a machine in no membrane. Say "" for a side you cannot
  tell.
- `places`: for a machine in a membrane, each of the diagram's other structures (`others`):
  "above" when it is the compartment above the membrane, "below" when it is the one below
  (the lesson's "cytoplasm" when the cytoplasm is below), "neither" for anything else (an
  ion, a molecule, a part the entry does not have). [] when there are none.

Use your knowledge of the machine: the subunit names in the entry, and what the course calls
its parts.
